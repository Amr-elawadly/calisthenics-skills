planche


The planche is an advanced calisthenics move where you hold your body parallel to the ground using only your hands for support. It requires exceptional strength, balance, and control, engaging the shoulders, arms, core, and wrists. Mastering the planche takes months or even years of dedicated training, as it demands both muscle endurance and proper technique. Progressions like the tuck planche, advanced tuck, and straddle planche help build the necessary strength and stability. Besides being a display of impressive athleticism, the planche improves overall body coordination and enhances pushing power, making it a valuable skill for calisthenics enthusiasts.


front lever


The front lever is a challenging calisthenics exercise where you hold your body straight and parallel to the ground while hanging from a bar or rings. It requires significant core, back, and arm strength to maintain the position without swinging or bending. This move engages the latissimus dorsi, core muscles, and posterior chain, making it an excellent test of full-body control. Achieving the front lever involves progressive training, starting with tuck and advanced tuck variations before moving to the full position. Besides showcasing impressive strength, the front lever enhances body awareness, grip endurance, and overall upper-body stability.


back lever


The back lever is a calisthenics skill where you hold your body straight and horizontal while hanging upside down from a bar or rings. It requires significant shoulder, core, and back strength, as well as flexibility to maintain the position without excessive strain. This move primarily engages the posterior chain, including the lower back, glutes, and shoulders, while also improving tendon resilience. Training for the back lever involves progressions like the tuck, advanced tuck, and straddle variations before achieving the full position. Besides demonstrating control and strength, mastering the back lever enhances shoulder mobility, stability, and overall body coordination.


90 degree hold


The 90-degree hold is an advanced calisthenics exercise where you hold a static position at the midpoint of a handstand push-up, with your elbows bent at a 90-degree angle and your body hovering above the ground. This move requires immense shoulder, triceps, and core strength, along with balance and endurance. It places intense pressure on the shoulders and requires strict form to prevent unnecessary strain. Training for the 90-degree hold involves building strength with pike push-ups, handstand holds, and controlled negatives. Mastering this skill improves pressing power, stability, and overall upper-body control, making it a valuable addition to advanced bodyweight training.
